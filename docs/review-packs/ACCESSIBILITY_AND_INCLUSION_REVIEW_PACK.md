## Known Risks

- Text-heavy consent, nested choices, time pressure, facilitator bias, or inaccessible rating scales.
- `Agency`, `shame`, `reactance`, `privacy`, and `recovery` do not translate or mean the same thing across groups.
- Accommodation needs become sensitive profile data or exclusion criteria.
- Small purposive samples produce tokenistic subgroup claims.
- Visual, hearing, motor, cognitive, literacy, trauma, and low-bandwidth needs are treated as one category.
- Other-support routes are not accessible or culturally appropriate.

## Specific Questions

1. What are the critical consent, skip, task, no-save/delete, exit, and support-route actions?
2. Which alternate formats, response modes, pacing, breaks, assistance, and communication supports are required?
3. Which words or concepts need cognitive interviewing before measurement?
4. How should accommodations be offered without forced disclosure?
5. What recruitment coverage is meaningful in formative rounds, and what claims remain prohibited?
6. How should accessibility failures be recorded and prioritized?
7. What localization and community review is required for English/German and later languages?

## Prohibited Assumptions

- Low fidelity, short text, plain language, or five decisions is automatically accessible.
- One disabled participant represents a disability group.
- Translation equals localization or cultural fit.
- Completion with facilitator correction is independent task success.
- Accessibility can be deferred until production.
