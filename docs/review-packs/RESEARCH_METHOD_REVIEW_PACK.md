## Known Risks

- Small-sample percentages create false precision.
- Think-aloud changes burden, privacy, and task behavior.
- Comparison order, facilitator support, or novelty biases results.
- Diverse recruitment goals become tokenistic or analytically underpowered.
- Post hoc threshold or theme changes inflate apparent readiness.
- Withdrawal/adverse events disappear from denominators.

## Specific Questions

1. What is the smallest defensible design for problem relevance, comprehension, task success, and adverse effects?
2. Should the comparator be paper, no tool/current coping, both, or neither?
3. Which sequence and counterbalancing method minimizes carryover while preserving safety?
4. Are 12-20 participants defensible; should cohorts or iterative rounds replace one pooled sample?
5. Which thresholds should be removed, changed to confidence intervals/ranges, or treated only as stop signals?
6. How should qualitative contradiction override numeric thresholds?
7. What independent ethics review and preregistration/version lock are proportionate?
8. How should accessibility and cultural-fit evidence be sampled and interpreted without universal claims?

## Prohibited Assumptions

- Formative percentages estimate population prevalence or effect size.
- Saturation is guaranteed by a fixed sample.
- Passing a threshold proves efficacy, clinical safety, or MVP readiness.
- Missing, skipped, withdrawn, or distressed participants may be excluded from decision denominators without disclosure.
- Founder or reviewer preference substitutes for participant evidence.
