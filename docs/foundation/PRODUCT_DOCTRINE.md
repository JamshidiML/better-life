# Better Life — Product Doctrine

Status: v0.1  
Date: 2026-07-10

This doctrine translates the founding vision into product decision rules. It should be used when evaluating features, architecture, copy, business models, partnerships, and growth tactics.

## 1. Core Product Principle

**Use calm-state consent to protect the user during high-risk states.**

The product may create strong friction and temporary restrictions, but those protections must originate from the adult user's informed, self-authored choices.

## 2. The Product Is A Capability System, Not A Punishment System

Better Life should help users:

- understand their patterns;
- reduce exposure and opportunity;
- interrupt automatic behavior;
- regulate emotion and attention;
- recover after lapses;
- strengthen self-efficacy;
- rely less on external control over time when appropriate.

The product must not:

- humiliate;
- threaten;
- expose;
- moralize by default;
- create public failure;
- punish lapses financially or socially;
- deliberately make the user feel broken.

## 3. Strong Protection Without Coercion

A strict mode can be legitimate when all of the following are true:

1. The user is an adult.
2. The user opts in voluntarily while calm.
3. The system explains what will be blocked, monitored, inferred, stored, and shared.
4. The user chooses the duration, strength, and support model.
5. The user understands platform limitations and bypass possibilities.
6. The system offers a pre-agreed modification and exit process.
7. No third party can secretly configure or extend restrictions.
8. The system does not claim that restriction alone is treatment.

## 4. The Precommitment Contract

The working product concept is a **Protection Constitution**: a user-authored agreement between the calm-state self and the future high-risk self.

It may define:

- personal reasons for change;
- protected times, places, devices, apps, and content paths;
- intervention intensity;
- delay periods before weakening protection;
- emergency or exceptional access rules;
- allowed data signals;
- local versus cloud processing;
- ally involvement;
- conditions for changing rules;
- review dates;
- deletion and exit rights.

The product must show the user the practical consequences before activation.

## 5. Layered Protection Model

No single blocker is sufficient. Better Life should evaluate a layered architecture.

### Layer A — User and life context

- values;
- goals;
- sleep;
- stress;
- isolation;
- work and travel patterns;
- relationship context;
- known high-risk windows.

### Layer B — Behavioral intervention

- delay;
- urge surfing;
- grounding;
- implementation intentions;
- replacement actions;
- environment change;
- social contact;
- recovery planning.

### Layer C — Application and browser friction

- browser extension;
- URL and domain controls;
- search and feed friction;
- app shielding where supported;
- safe redirects;
- controlled override paths.

### Layer D — Device and operating-system controls

- platform-provided parental/self-control APIs where appropriate;
- device policy capabilities where appropriate and consented;
- local VPN or filtering mechanisms where technically and legally feasible;
- desktop agents;
- tamper awareness without deceptive surveillance.

### Layer E — Network protection

- DNS and router guidance;
- optional managed network profiles;
- household and travel modes;
- transparent limitations for VPNs, cellular networks, alternate devices, and encrypted traffic.

### Layer F — Human support

- trusted ally;
- coach;
- therapist;
- peer group;
- crisis or professional-care routing.

Human support must use the minimum useful disclosure.

### Layer G — Intelligence

- personalized risk patterns;
- sequence detection;
- intervention selection;
- local-first inference where possible;
- user-visible explanations;
- uncertainty and appeal paths;
- no hidden diagnosis.

## 6. Prevention, Support, Repair

Every major flow should address three moments:

### Before

- identify risk;
- reduce exposure;
- prepare the environment;
- make healthy actions easy;
- strengthen the user's plan.

### During

- create a pause;
- increase friction;
- reduce stimulation;
- guide attention and environment;
- offer a chosen support pathway.

### After

- avoid shame escalation;
- identify the sequence;
- repair immediate harm;
- update the protection plan;
- recommend professional help when indicated.

## 7. Simple Outside, Sophisticated Inside

The user experience should be understandable in minutes even if the underlying system is technically complex.

Design targets:

- plain language;
- progressive disclosure;
- few decisions at first setup;
- strong defaults with clear explanations;
- one primary action during a high-risk moment;
- accessible visual and interaction design;
- multilingual and culturally adaptable language;
- no unnecessary clinical jargon;
- no dashboard that turns recovery into obsessive self-surveillance.

## 8. Privacy Doctrine

- Collect the minimum necessary data.
- Prefer on-device processing for intimate signals.
- Separate identity, contact, research, and behavioral data.
- Make data flows visible and understandable.
- Encrypt sensitive data in transit and at rest.
- Use short retention where long retention is unnecessary.
- Allow export and deletion.
- Do not sell intimate data.
- Do not use intimate data for advertising.
- Do not train general models on raw intimate user data without a separate, explicit, expert-reviewed consent and governance process.
- Treat inferred sexual, health, emotional, religious, relationship, and behavioral information as highly sensitive.

## 9. Clinical Honesty Doctrine

The product may use evidence-informed techniques, but it must distinguish between:

- self-help;
- behavioral support;
- coaching;
- psychotherapy;
- diagnosis;
- medical treatment;
- emergency response.

The application should not diagnose users or imply that all unwanted pornography use is a clinical disorder.

Product claims must match the evidence actually available for the specific feature, population, and outcome.

## 10. Artificial Intelligence Doctrine

AI is a component, not the product identity.

Use AI only when it adds measurable value over simpler methods.

Preferred uses:

- organizing user-authored plans;
- adapting intervention suggestions;
- summarizing de-identified patterns;
- explaining trends;
- selecting among approved support flows;
- helping users prepare non-explicit summaries for professionals;
- local risk estimation when feasible and validated.

Prohibited or high-risk uses without dedicated review:

- diagnosis from behavioral traces;
- inferring sexuality, trauma, religion, or mental illness without necessity and explicit consent;
- hidden emotion recognition;
- manipulating users through personalized shame or fear;
- opaque automated decisions that restrict users without explanation;
- sending raw intimate content or histories to external models by default.

## 11. Evidence Doctrine

For every major feature, document:

- the user problem;
- the proposed mechanism;
- supporting evidence;
- quality and limitations of evidence;
- potential harms;
- data required;
- consent model;
- technical feasibility;
- testable success criteria;
- conditions for removal or redesign.

A plausible idea is not evidence.

## 12. Platform Honesty Doctrine

Never market a stronger level of control than the platform can reliably provide.

For each platform, document:

- what can be blocked;
- what can be delayed;
- what can be detected;
- what requires special permissions;
- what conflicts with VPNs, browsers, app-store rules, device ownership, enterprise management, or operating-system policies;
- what can be bypassed;
- what happens when protection fails.

## 13. Business Doctrine

Better Life should not profit from longer suffering.

Avoid:

- advertising based on intimate behavior;
- selling behavioral profiles;
- shame-driven upselling;
- deliberately weakening the free version during high-risk moments;
- addictive engagement mechanics;
- exploiting partner anxiety or surveillance demand.

Potential sustainable models:

- low-cost membership;
- voluntary supporter membership;
- grants and public-interest funding;
- research partnerships;
- NGO partnerships;
- ethically designed professional services;
- optional paid advanced infrastructure with a strong free core.

## 14. Feature Decision Scorecard

A feature advances only when it has acceptable answers to:

1. Does it solve a repeated, important user problem?
2. Is the mechanism credible?
3. Does evidence support the mechanism or justify a careful experiment?
4. Is it understandable to the user?
5. Is consent meaningful?
6. Is the required data proportionate?
7. Can it be built honestly on the target platform?
8. What are the coercion, privacy, shame, false-positive, and false-confidence risks?
9. Can the user challenge, modify, or leave it safely?
10. Can we test benefit without overclaiming?

## 15. North Star

The strongest Better Life product is not the one that controls users forever. It is the one that helps users protect themselves during vulnerable moments, build a life with fewer destructive loops, and gradually regain durable freedom.
