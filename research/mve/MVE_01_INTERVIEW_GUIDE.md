# MVE-01 Interview Guide

- Version: `0.2-draft`
- Status: Draft for specialist/ethics/plain-language review; do not administer
- Interview type: Bounded formative product interview, not clinical interview

## Facilitator Boundary

Do not diagnose, screen for disorder, assess treatment need, interpret behavior, provide therapy, act as sponsor/peer, validate abstinence, assess calmness/capacity, or promise emergency response. Do not ask for explicit content, websites/apps used, frequency, sexual history, trauma, illegal behavior, third-party identity, or proof.

Use these controls throughout:

- `You can answer broadly, skip this, take a break, or stop.`
- `I do not need explicit details or another person's information.`
- `I can explain the research material, but I cannot provide clinical or emergency support.`

## Opening and Teach-Back

1. In your own words, what do you think this research is about?
2. What does the draft tool do, and what does it not do?
3. What can you do if you do not want to answer or continue?
4. What is the difference between choices shown in the prototype and research records collected by the study?

Stop and clarify if the participant believes the tool diagnoses, treats, monitors, blocks, contacts someone, uses AI, or guarantees privacy/deletion. Persistent misunderstanding of a critical boundary ends the session.

## Problem Relevance

1. Does the broad idea of wanting help with a self-chosen goal feel relevant, not relevant, or unclear to you?
2. What words make the idea feel practical, moralizing, clinical, ideological, or confusing?
3. Is there a safer or simpler alternative you would prefer, including no tool?

Do not ask what explicit behavior occurred. If context is needed, invite a broad label or `prefer not to say`.

## Five Decisions

For each decision, ask the participant to think aloud only if that method is approved and comfortable:

1. What do you think this question is asking?
2. Which options feel missing, pressuring, or too personal?
3. Can you skip or choose `prefer not to say`? Show how.
4. What do you expect the choice to change?

After all five:

- What did you choose for help/goal, context, action, fallback, and keep/use-once/discard?
- Which choice was least necessary or most burdensome?
- Did any question feel like diagnosis, confession, abstinence pressure, or a promise of protection?

## Manual Protective Spiral

1. What did you expect `Guide me` to do?
2. Was the action useful, irrelevant, too obvious, too demanding, or uncomfortable?
3. Did it support your choice or feel like pressure?
4. Was the fallback clear and genuinely optional?
5. Would no tool, paper, another private approach, or professional/existing support fit better?

Do not ask whether the action prevented a behavior or constituted treatment success.

## Privacy and Control

1. What do you believe is stored by the draft tool? Where?
2. What does `use once`, `save locally`, and `discard` mean to you?
3. What research information do you think the study keeps separately?
4. How would you close, discard, withdraw, or ask about deletion?
5. What could make local or paper material unsafe in a shared device/home/context?
6. Which data, recording, analytics, AI, partner, or contact use would make you decline?

Do not confirm technical deletion/privacy behavior that has not been implemented and tested.

## Agency, Burden, Shame, and Reactance

1. At any point did you feel more in control, less in control, or unchanged?
2. Did the flow feel burdensome, repetitive, urgent, or hard to leave?
3. Did any word or action cause shame, judgment, pressure, anger, resistance, or a wish to do the opposite?
4. Did it increase rumination, distress, reassurance-seeking, or concern about privacy?
5. Did it imply that using a stronger mode, professional care, abstinence, or disclosure was expected?

Pause and follow the adverse-event plan for material distress or privacy regret. Do not probe for a story.

## Accessibility and Cultural Fit

1. Could you perceive, understand, and act on every critical choice using your preferred format and accommodations?
2. What wording, sequence, rating scale, or time expectation was inaccessible?
3. Did the flow assume a culture, faith or secular view, sexuality/gender, family structure, literacy, ability, or recovery identity?
4. What would make this easier or safer without requiring sensitive disclosure?
5. Would another language, format, pace, response mode, or human support be needed?

Do not treat one participant as representative of a group.

## Exit, Deletion, and Other Support

1. Please show how you would close or discard the draft.
2. What do you expect to remain afterward in the prototype and in research records?
3. Where would you go if this tool did not fit or you wanted other support?
4. Did the other-support route imply endorsement, clinical assessment, or emergency response?

Any inability to exit or critical deletion misunderstanding is recorded and triggers the stop rule.

## Preference and Reuse

1. Which did you prefer: MVE-01, paper, no tool/current coping, or none? Why?
2. Would you voluntarily use a reviewed version again? Under what conditions?
3. What result would convince you the concept should be stopped rather than expanded?
4. What is the smallest change worth testing next?

## Closing

- Is there anything about burden, shame, pressure, privacy, accessibility, cultural fit, or safety that we did not ask?
- Do you want any optional recording or quote withdrawn, subject to the disclosed processing stage and limits?
- Reconfirm prototype disposal, research-record status, compensation, follow-up choice, and researcher limits.

## Note Discipline

Record coded answers, task observations, and short paraphrases where possible. Do not record unnecessary exact dates, locations, employers, third-party identities, rare events, explicit details, URLs, app names, sexual history, or raw narrative. No raw notes or recordings enter GitHub or an external AI system.
