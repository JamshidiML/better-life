## Founder Decision on EUR1

Founder decision: `EUR1` is an accessibility principle and testable anchor. It is not a binding product constraint, checkout price, monthly billing promise, forecast, or reason to underfund safety, privacy, evidence, accessibility, maintenance, support, or shutdown.

Research must compare free, EUR1 framing, low annual price, supporter funding, sponsored access, and no-payment options for comprehension, affordability, dignity, willingness, cancellation, and perceived quality. Full cost and safety determine whether a paid layer can exist; essential safety/privacy rights remain free.

## Decision Frame

| Required question | Cycle 3 answer |
| --- | --- |
| User problem | Financial models may exclude users, underfund safety, monetize vulnerability, or reward engagement rather than recovery-relevant benefit. |
| Expected benefit | Separated funding, impact, reward, and growth models may preserve access and accountability while testing sustainable value. |
| Supporting evidence | Official guidance can inform specific incentive, campaign, consumer, and nonprofit-program questions. It does not establish Better Life demand, impact, reward effectiveness, savings, eligibility, or partnership. |
| Required data | Aggregated cost/funding/payment/support data separated from intimate product data; protocol-specific outcomes; minimum non-sensitive benefit proof only if approved. |
| Consent requirements | Payment, supporter status, reward participation, research, impact evaluation, partner benefit, analytics, model training, and public reporting are separate. |
| Safety risks | Underfunded operations, motivation crowding, shame, inequity, care displacement, compulsive reward seeking, and exclusion. |
| Misuse risks | Vulnerability pricing/targeting, intimate proof, partner surveillance, pay-to-rank, fraud, endorsement confusion, inflated savings, and mission capture. |
| Platform feasibility | Local free MVE has low variable infrastructure but real research, review, accessibility, legal, support, maintenance, and exit costs. Rewards/partners add unvalidated operations. |
| Success metric | Full-cost coverage by layer; free-core preservation; user value/affordability; safety coverage; no intimate-data monetization; validated outcomes with harms; orderly exit. |
| Exit strategy | Stop reward/partner/campaign/paid layer, honor earned benefits/refunds/rights, preserve free core and care routes, remove data/access, disclose horizon, and avoid sponsor lock-in. |

## Theory of Change

```text
Inputs -> Activities -> Outputs -> Short-term outcomes -> Long-term outcomes -> Social/economic impact
```

| Link | Hypothesis | Evidence/measurement needed | Failure/exit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Inputs | Research funding, independent expertise, reviewed content, accessible design, privacy/security capacity, and founder time can support a responsible experiment. | Quotes, conflicts, loaded labor, opportunity cost, runway, and governance. | Stop/narrow when required expertise or safety reserve is unfunded. |
| Activities | Optional Supportive planning, transparent microlearning, private reflection, accurate routing, and governed research may help. | Fidelity, exposure, opt-out, burden, adverse effects, and comparison condition. | Remove any activity with no incremental benefit or material harm. |
| Outputs | Completed optional plan/action, viewed explanation, requested resource, repaired plan, or consented research response. | Denominators and missingness; output is not outcome. | Do not optimize completion/DAU as recovery. |
| Short-term outcomes | Agency, comprehension, fit, reduced shame/burden, helpful replacement action, earlier help-seeking, and successful recovery/exit may improve. | Validated/user-centered measures, counterfactual, subgroup effects, adverse outcomes. | Suspend claims/intervention if harm or null evidence dominates. |
| Long-term outcomes | Quality of life, functioning, reduced harm, sustained support, family wellbeing, connection, and appropriate care access may improve. | Prospective controlled evidence, follow-up, confounding/attrition, clinical/public-health review. | No causal claim without method; retire unsupported measures. |
| Social/economic impact | Healthcare, productivity, family, public-service, and digital-wellbeing value may exist. | Transparent cost-of-illness boundary, attribution, displacement, equity, sensitivity, double-count checks. | No avoided-cost/SROI claim until independently validated. |

Repository decision: human life, dignity, survival, or suffering is not assigned a monetary value. Cost and quality-of-life methods may inform resource decisions only under approved methodology with uncertainty and equity visible.

## Impact Measurement Architecture

| Outcome family | Candidate measure | Claim ceiling | Required reviewer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Agency/control | User-reported choice, plan fit, recovery/exit success | Product-usefulness hypothesis | Method, lived experience, accessibility |
| Quality of life/function | Validated optional measure after review | Association unless controlled causal design | Clinical, method, privacy |
| Harm/adverse effect | Shame, distress, rumination, coercion, lockout, care delay, privacy regret | Safety finding within protocol | Clinical/safety, ethics |
| Help-seeking/access | User-reported route use and barriers | No treatment effect | Clinical/public health |
| Connection | User-defined support adequacy, isolation, adverse social effect | No peer-network benefit transfer | Community/safeguarding |
| Family/productivity/cost | Explicitly consented research measures, not employer/admin product telemetry | No savings or causal claim | Public health, economist, labor/privacy |
| Product integrity | Consent, deletion, recovery, expiry, teardown, incident outcomes | System quality only | Privacy/security/accessibility |

Impact work costs include instrument licensing where applicable, translation/accessibility, participant compensation, ethics, recruitment, follow-up, data management, independent analysis, adverse-event response, publication, and long-term retention/deletion obligations. They are not hidden inside feature engineering.

## Reward Safety and Economics

Rewards are excluded from the first MVE. A reward candidate must document:

| Field | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Target behavior | Agency, learning, help-seeking, safe repair, healthy replacement, privacy/recovery rehearsal, or bounded contribution; never abstinence perfection or daily use. |
| Direct evidence | Exact population, intervention, amount/type, comparator, duration, outcome, harms, and transfer distance. |
| Economics | Benefit face value, funding source, issuance/verification/support/fraud/tax cost, breakage, liability, expiry, refunds, and shutdown reserve. |
| Motivation | Intrinsic/extrinsic rationale, crowding-out risk, dependence, stopping behavior, no-reward comparator. |
| Proof/data | Minimum non-intimate proof; no plan/history/content/mode/stage/setback/care/surveillance proof. |
| Consent/equity | Optional, revocable, accessible, fair across income/disability/geography/time, not required for essential support. |
| Partner | No intimate data, user control, care influence, rank, endorsement implication, sole dependency, or advertising audience. |
| Legal | Tax, consumer, sanctions, charity, employment, promotion, health, gambling/financial, and jurisdiction review. |
| Stop | Fraud, coercion, shame, compulsive use, inequity, disclosure pressure, care/access interference, or no incremental benefit. |

Evidence-supported conclusion: contingency management has evidence and official guidance in specific substance-use treatment contexts. It does not validate Better Life badges, credits, tokens, discounts, or app rewards. Source: https://www.samhsa.gov/resource/ebp/using-samhsa-funds-implement-evidence-based-contingency-management-services

Prohibited rewards: public leaderboard/shame, streak-loss punishment, gambling-like randomness, intimate disclosure, stronger surveillance, payer status as recovery status, essential-support withholding, DAU optimization, token speculation, and unpaid safety labor.

## Ethical Growth Economics

```text
Awareness -> Trust -> First value -> Voluntary return -> Learning -> Support -> Outcome -> Advocacy
```

| Stage | Permitted metric candidate | Guardrail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Awareness | Reach among a broad opted/appropriate audience, source accuracy | No inferred-vulnerability targeting, intimate lookalikes, fear/shame/diagnosis advertising. |
| Trust | Comprehension, source credibility, privacy/claim understanding | No endorsements/clinical claims not earned; no artificial urgency. |
| First value | User-defined usefulness and burden from a one-time local experience | No account, payment, disclosure, reward, or notification required for MVE. |
| Voluntary return | User-chosen return intent, not DAU target | No streaks, variable-ratio loops, loss framing, or pressure. |
| Learning/support | Optional comprehension and route usefulness | Education is transparent; no hidden persuasion or care replacement. |
| Outcome | Agency/fit/harm/help-seeking under approved protocol | No output/engagement proxy or causal inflation. |
| Advocacy | Voluntary recommendation with no exposed recovery status | No referral pressure, intimate testimonial incentive, or public social proof without rights. |

Source-verified fact: WHO publishes behavior-change communication guidance. Hypothesis: selected campaign principles may inform Better Life only after ethics, cultural, user, and outcome review: https://www.who.int/about/communications/actionable/behaviour-change

Every channel budget includes creative/source rights, cultural/accessibility review, privacy/legal review, moderation/response, measurement, adverse-event monitoring, and shutdown. Growth spending cannot outrank safety remediation or independent evidence.

## First MVE Boundary
