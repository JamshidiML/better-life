## Decision Frame

| Required question | Cycle 3 answer |
| --- | --- |
| User problem | Users may need a short personal plan and, later, an optional pathway that adapts to their explicit preferences without assigning identity, diagnosis, or one recovery ideology. |
| Expected benefit | A small plan may support action; an adaptive pathway may improve fit and learning. Both are unvalidated Better Life hypotheses. |
| Supporting evidence | Evidence-supported conclusion: self-management, education, values, and reflection mechanisms can be researched cautiously. No evidence establishes this pathway or reward system. |
| Required data | First MVE: five local user decisions only. Future path: explicit preferences, stage/version, exercises, user-authored reflections, safety choices, review/expiry, and purpose-specific receipts. |
| Consent requirements | Setup is user-initiated at a chosen time with voluntary readiness confirmation and comprehension. Sensitive variants, sharing, research, analytics, and model training are separate and optional. |
| Safety risks | Shame, rumination, identity pressure, unsafe repair/contact, care displacement, compulsive tracking, cognitive burden, and accessibility exclusion. |
| Misuse risks | Sensitive inference, partner/employer authorship, reward coercion, surveillance, theology/anti-theology, abstinence enforcement, and stale cross-device policy. |
| Platform feasibility | A local five-decision form is feasible to prototype. Cross-device sync, adaptive engines, rewards, licensed paths, and model learning are unvalidated future systems. |
| Success metric | Completion/burden, decision recall, perceived agency/fit, shame/adverse effects, skip/exit success, name comprehension, and correct conflict handling. |
| Exit strategy | Skip, defer, revise, repeat, archive, export, delete, reset adaptation, choose no-save, exit the pathway, and retain direct access to safety/recovery/care. |

## First Minimum Viable Experiment: Five Decisions

`MVE` means `Minimum Viable Experiment`.

The first MVE is a small, local, accountless, Supportive-only, non-clinical form. It requires no more than these five user decisions:

| Decision | Prompt intent | Minimum stored value | Optional, not required |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. `What do you want help with right now?` | Choose a neutral user-authored goal or `Prefer not to say`. | Short local label/category. | Explicit detail, diagnosis, identity, history. |
| 2. `When would guidance be useful?` | Choose a broad self-named context or `Any time I open it`. | Local context label. | Behavioral monitoring, risk prediction, location. |
| 3. `What is one action you want available?` | Choose one reviewed Supportive action or exit. | Action ID. | Custom sensitive narrative. |
| 4. `What should happen if that action does not fit?` | Choose one fallback: another action, close, or external resource. | Fallback ID. | Peer, ally, enforcement, automatic contact. |
| 5. `Keep this plan?` | Confirm local save until review, one-time/no-save use, or discard. | Save choice and optional review/expiry. | Account, sync, analytics, rewards, model training. |

Repository decision: declining, skipping, or discarding any answer never limits safety, recovery, exit, care, or essential support. No field is converted into diagnosis, risk score, capacity judgment, or mode eligibility.

## Naming Research Protocol

Candidate names are hypotheses: `My plan`, `Personal plan`, `Protection plan`, `Support plan`, and a participant-suggested neutral name. `Recovery Plan` and `Protection Constitution` may be tested only as comparison candidates because they may imply diagnosis, ideology, legal force, severity, or permanence.

Measure unaided interpretation, expected contents, clinical meaning, control/ownership, permanence, shame, cultural/faith fit, translation, accessibility, and preferred alternative. Stop a candidate if a material subgroup interprets it as diagnosis, compulsory abstinence, partner control, contract, treatment, confession, or proof of recovery.

No user-facing canonical name is approved in Cycle 3.

## Future Living Pathway Schema

This schema is future architecture, not the first MVE.

| Field | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Stage identity | Stable ID/version, Track A licensed/authorized or Track B original, source/provenance, author, and status. |
| Purpose and user need | Specific expected benefit and evidence/source; founder experience is separately classified. |
| Evidence | Classification, certainty, transfer distance, population/applicability, contradiction, and expiry. |
| Harms/contraindications | Shame, rumination, trauma, unsafe contact, coercion, care displacement, accessibility, and cultural exclusion. |
| Variants | User-chosen secular, spiritual, cultural, language, literacy, sensory, cognitive, voice/text, depth, and no-save variants. |
| Exercise/reflection | Original or authorized content, time/burden, data need, stop rule, and non-clinical boundary. |
| Optional human support | User-chosen professional/external/community route with visible role; no AI/human-peer/sponsor/clinician/emergency impersonation. |
| Completion evidence | User-chosen reflection/action or `not applicable`; never a truth, abstinence, severity, or recovery score. |
| Choice | `Skip`, `Defer`, `Revise`, `Repeat`, `Exit`, and change variant without penalty. |
| Clinical/safety boundary | Unsafe-contact alternatives, direct care access, emergency limits, and escalation owner. |
| Reward eligibility | Allowed/blocked rationale under reward-safety framework; no intimate disclosure or essential-access effect. |
| Data minimization | Minimum data, location, visibility, no-save, export, delete, reset learning, purpose, consent, retention. |
| Review/expiry | Evidence, rights, clinical, safeguarding, cultural, accessibility, and product reviewers; expiry/retirement trigger. |

## Choice-Driven Adaptation

Allowed adaptation inputs are explicit user selections such as language, secular/spiritual preference, content depth, voice/text, accessibility options, preferred exercise format, selected stage, and `show less/more like this`.

Prohibited inferred inputs include religion, sexuality, relationship form, diagnosis, trauma, criminality, moral worth, abstinence status, cognitive capacity, mental state, relapse risk, and protected traits. Time, device, routine, occupation, household, professional-care context, and support network require a defined benefit and separate voluntary input; absence is not inferred.

Every future adaptation rule must be user-visible, editable, resettable, deletable, explainable, and independently testable. No external model is needed for the first MVE.

## Stage Choice and Completion Boundaries

- Stages are optional tools, not a required sequence, recovery rank, or eligibility ladder.
- The user may skip, defer, revise, repeat, reorder, switch Track B variants, or exit without failure language.
- Track A content cannot be mixed into Track B without rights/provenance and visible source boundaries.
- Inventory, responsibility, and repair never require confession, apology, contact with unsafe people, disclosure of trauma, self-incrimination, or a specific theology/anti-theology.
- A no-contact repair may include private learning, changed behavior, professional advice, or a safely bounded indirect action.
- Stage completion never activates/escalates a mode, extends protection, changes recovery, unlocks care, changes payment, or determines essential support.

## Reward Safety Boundary

Rewards are excluded from the first MVE. A future candidate requires:

| Field | Required question |
| --- | --- |
| Target behavior | Does it reinforce agency, learning, help-seeking, repair, healthy replacement, or safe contribution rather than abstinence perfection or engagement? |
| Evidence | What direct evidence supports reward use, and what motivation/crowding-out uncertainty remains? |
| Consent | Is participation separate, revocable, and unrelated to essential support? |
| Gaming/coercion | Can it pressure disclosure, surveillance, repeated use, partner control, fraud, or unpaid safety labor? |
| Equity/accessibility | Can people with disability, low connectivity, low income, different cultures, or limited time participate fairly? |
| Data/partner | What minimum proof is needed; partner receives no intimate data and no recovery status. |
| Legal/tax | What consumer, tax, sanctions, promotion, employment, health, and jurisdiction questions require counsel? |
| Expiry/stop | When does the reward end, and what adverse effect immediately suspends it? |

Prohibited: public leaderboards/shame; streak-loss punishment; random/gambling-like rewards; intimate-disclosure rewards; stronger-surveillance rewards; payer status as recovery status; withholding essential support; daily-active-use optimization; and financially compulsive tokens.

Private acknowledgement may be researched only after the pathway itself shows usefulness and no material harm. It cannot change mode, stage, care, recovery, or access.

## Cross-Device Version Conflict Rules

The first MVE has no account or sync. For any future sync:

1. Every policy/path version has immutable ID, device/session origin, parent version, timestamp, consent version, and content hash.
2. Concurrent edits never resolve by silent last-write-wins.
3. A conflict freezes the affected new enforcement/change, not essential access or safe recovery.
4. The user sees both human-readable versions, affected fields, data location, and current device state.
5. The user chooses `keep this`, `keep other`, `merge safe non-authority fields`, `keep local only`, or `teardown sync`.
6. Deletion, withdrawal, reduced sharing, or safer recovery wins temporarily over stale expansion until the user resolves explicitly.
7. Old consent cannot authorize new purpose, recipient, retention, adapter, ally, or data field.
8. All devices receive the chosen resolution or show degraded/conflict state; unresolved devices cannot claim current protection.

Hypothesis: these rules are safer than automatic merge. They require Thread 07 data/security architecture and executed conflict tests.

## Mode Interface

Thread 05 owns modes/transitions (`BL-MODE-01`). The pathway may offer a user-selected Supportive action but cannot assign or escalate modes. Exact current mode copy is:

- Supportive: `Guide me`
- Friction: `Help me pause`
- Strict: `Protect the decision I made while calm`

`Calm` is never inferred. Strict requires user-chosen setup time, voluntary readiness confirmation, comprehension, and recovery rehearsal. The first MVE includes Supportive only.
